BitOptions Protocol

Decentralized Weekly Options Trading on Solana
📄 Version 1.0 📅 March 2026 🔗 bitoptions.xyz
Abstract

BitOptions is a non-custodial, permissionless options protocol deployed on the Solana blockchain. The protocol enables users to buy weekly call and put options on Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana — settled in USDT — without accounts, identity verification, or centralized intermediaries. A shared liquidity pool model replaces traditional order books, providing consistent liquidity from day one. Dynamic pricing mechanisms and notional caps protect liquidity providers from directional risk while enabling fair, transparent pricing for all participants.

Section 01

Introduction

Crypto options markets have historically been dominated by centralized exchanges — Deribit, OKX, Bybit — each requiring account creation, identity verification, and custody of user funds. These platforms impose geo-restrictions that exclude large portions of the global population, charge opaque fees, and introduce counterparty risk that contradicts the trustless ethos of decentralized finance.

Existing DeFi options protocols have attempted to address these shortcomings but often introduce excessive complexity — multiple vault types, complex tokenomics, and UX that alienates all but the most sophisticated traders. The result is a market with genuine demand and inadequate supply of accessible solutions.

BitOptions is designed around a single principle: radical simplicity without sacrificing integrity. Round strike prices. Weekly expirations. One-click trading. USDT settlement. No custody. No KYC. The protocol handles everything else on-chain.

Key Design Goals

  • Accessibility: Any wallet, anywhere, any time
  • Simplicity: Clean strikes, weekly expiries, no complexity theater
  • Transparency: All pricing, collateral, and settlement on-chain
  • Security: Non-custodial, audited, oracle-protected
  • Capital efficiency: Shared liquidity pools maximize LP yield
Section 02

Options Fundamentals

2.1 Calls and Puts

An option is a financial contract that grants the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price (the strike price) on or before a specified date (the expiry).

  • Call Option: The right to buy the underlying asset at the strike price. Profitable when the asset price rises above the strike.
  • Put Option: The right to sell the underlying asset at the strike price. Profitable when the asset price falls below the strike.

The buyer pays a premium upfront to acquire this right. The seller (in BitOptions' case, the liquidity pool) collects the premium and assumes the obligation to pay if the option expires in-the-money.

BitOptions uses European-style cash settlement: options can only be exercised at expiry, and payouts are made in USDT rather than delivering the underlying asset.

2.2 Option Pricing — The Black-Scholes Model

The industry-standard framework for pricing European options is the Black-Scholes-Merton model (1973). The theoretical price of a call option is given by:

C = S·N(d₁) − K·e−rT·N(d₂) P = K·e−rT·N(−d₂) − S·N(−d₁) where: d₁ = [ ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)·T ] / (σ·√T) d₂ = d₁ − σ·√T S = Current spot price of the underlying asset K = Strike price T = Time to expiry (in years) r = Risk-free interest rate σ = Implied volatility (annualized) N = Cumulative standard normal distribution function

BitOptions uses a modified form of this model with on-chain implied volatility parameters updated periodically by protocol governance, supplemented by an imbalance adjustment mechanism described in Section 5.

2.3 The Greeks

The "Greeks" measure an option's sensitivity to various factors and are essential for understanding risk:

Δ
Delta
Rate of change of option price relative to the underlying asset price. Ranges from 0 to 1 for calls, −1 to 0 for puts. ATM options have delta ≈ 0.5.
Γ
Gamma
Rate of change of Delta relative to the underlying price. Measures convexity. Highest for ATM options near expiry.
Θ
Theta
Rate of time decay — how much the option loses in value per day, all else equal. Always negative for buyers; a key driver of LP returns.
ν
Vega
Sensitivity of option price to changes in implied volatility. A 1% increase in IV increases the option value by Vega dollars.

2.4 Weekly Options

BitOptions offers exclusively weekly expirations, settling every Friday at 08:00 UTC. This design choice reflects several market realities:

  • Event-driven trading: Macro events, Fed announcements, and protocol upgrades often have known dates. Weekly options enable targeted positioning.
  • Accelerated theta decay: Weekly options decay faster than monthlies, increasing the statistical advantage for liquidity providers.
  • Capital efficiency: Traders can deploy capital in weekly cycles rather than locking it up for months.
  • Reduced tail risk: Shorter duration limits the pool's exposure to sustained directional moves.

The BitOptions Protocol

3.1 Protocol Overview

BitOptions is implemented as a suite of smart contracts on the Solana blockchain, chosen for its sub-second finality, transaction costs measured in fractions of a cent, and mature DeFi ecosystem. The protocol comprises four core programs:

3.3 Settlement Mechanics

BitOptions uses cash-settled European-style expiry. At each Friday 08:00 UTC expiry:

  1. The Oracle Program computes a 30-minute TWAP from Pyth price data (07:30–08:00 UTC) to establish the settlement price S_T
  2. For each open option, the settlement payout is calculated:
Call Payout = max(0, S_T − K) × contract_size Put Payout = max(0, K − S_T) × contract_size S_T = settlement price at expiry K = strike price
  1. Payouts are denominated in USDT and transferred directly to the option holder's wallet
  2. Remaining collateral is released back to the liquidity pool
  3. Premium income collected during the week remains in the pool as LP yield
TWAP Protection: The 30-minute time-weighted average price prevents last-minute price manipulation at settlement. Even a large market order cannot meaningfully move a 30-minute TWAP on a liquid market.
Section 04

Liquidity Pool Architecture

4.1 The Shared Liquidity Pool

Traditional options exchanges match buyers with sellers through an order book, requiring both sides to be present simultaneously. This creates liquidity fragmentation and wide bid-ask spreads in emerging markets.

BitOptions replaces the order book with a shared liquidity pool — a single USDT reserve that acts as the counterparty to every option buyer. Liquidity providers (LPs) deposit USDT into the pool and collectively assume the short-option position across all open contracts.

This model provides:

  • Guaranteed liquidity for any order within pool capacity limits
  • Uniform pricing for all participants at any given moment
  • Passive yield for LPs through premium income
  • No need to bootstrap two-sided liquidity at launch

4.2 LP Token Mechanics

When a user deposits USDT, they receive LP tokens representing their proportional share of the pool. LP token price floats based on pool performance:

LP Token Price = Total Pool Value / Total LP Token Supply Tokens Minted = Deposit Amount / Current LP Token Price USDT Returned = LP Tokens × Current LP Token Price

As premiums accumulate and options expire worthless, the pool grows and LP token price increases. If payouts exceed premiums in a period, LP token price decreases proportionally. LPs can withdraw at any time, subject to available capital minimums.

4.3 Capital Utilization and Collateral

For each option sold, the Vault Program locks collateral equal to the maximum possible payout. This ensures the pool can always honor every open contract:

Available Capital = Total Deposits − Locked Collateral Utilization Rate = Locked Collateral / Total Deposits Call Max Payout = (Price_ceiling − K) × size // capped at 3× strike Put Max Payout = K × size // asset cannot go below 0

Target utilization is 40–60%. Below 40%, the pool is under-deployed; above 60%, the imbalance multiplier begins pricing new options at a premium to discourage further concentration (Section 5.2).

Dynamic Pricing Model

5.1 Base Premium

The base premium for each option is derived from the Black-Scholes formula using on-chain parameters. Implied volatility (σ) is maintained per-asset by protocol governance and updated based on realized 30-day volatility:

5.2 Imbalance Adjustment

When the pool becomes skewed toward one side (e.g., too many calls sold relative to puts), the pricing model increases the cost of adding further imbalance. This serves as a self-correcting mechanism that stabilizes pool exposure:

One-Side UtilizationMultiplier (μ)Effect
0 – 20%1.0×No adjustment
20 – 40%1.25×Slight premium
40 – 60%1.75×Moderate premium
60 – 80%2.5×High premium
> 80%4.0×Prohibitive — effectively capped
Final Premium = BS_Premium × μ(utilization) × SizeFactor

5.3 Size Impact

Large orders receive slightly less favorable pricing to prevent pool concentration risk from single actors:

SizeFactor = 1 + (Order Notional / Available Capital) × 0.10

A $100,000 order in a $1,000,000 pool carries a 1% size premium. A $500,000 order carries a 5% premium. This creates natural incentive to split large orders across time.

Section 06

Risk Management

6.1 Notional Caps

Hard limits on pool exposure are enforced at the contract level and cannot be overridden:

  • Maximum one-directional exposure per asset: 40% of pool capital
  • Maximum total per-asset exposure (calls + puts combined): 60% of pool capital
  • Orders that would breach these limits are rejected before any funds transfer

6.2 Oracle Security

Price manipulation at settlement is a known attack vector in DeFi options protocols. BitOptions mitigates this through:

  • Pyth Network feeds: High-frequency, manipulation-resistant price aggregation from institutional market makers
  • 30-minute TWAP: Settlement price is time-weighted, making last-minute manipulation economically impractical
  • Secondary oracle fallback: A backup oracle source is queried if the primary feed is stale or unavailable at expiry

6.3 Smart Contract Risk

  • Full audit by OtterSec prior to mainnet deployment
  • Ongoing bug bounty program with tiered rewards
  • Upgradeable proxy architecture with 48-hour governance timelock on all changes
  • Emergency pause functionality controlled by multi-sig (3-of-5) during initial period
