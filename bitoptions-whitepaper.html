Abstract

BitOptions is a permissionless, non-custodial options protocol built on Solana that enables traders to buy weekly call and put options on Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana — fully settled in USDT. By replacing centralized intermediaries with smart contracts, shared liquidity pools, and Pyth Network oracle feeds, BitOptions delivers transparent on-chain options trading with no custodial risk, no geographic restrictions, and minimal complexity. This paper describes the protocol architecture, pricing model, liquidity mechanics, and risk framework underlying the BitOptions platform.

Section 01

Introduction

The global options market represents one of the largest and most actively traded financial instruments in traditional finance. Yet in the cryptocurrency space, access to options trading has remained largely gated behind centralized exchanges (CEXs) — platforms that impose custodial risk, demand identity verification, enforce geographic restrictions, and maintain opaque pricing models.

The Problem

Existing crypto options markets suffer from several structural weaknesses:

  • Custodial risk: Users must deposit funds with a centralized entity, creating single points of failure and counterparty exposure.
  • Geographic barriers: Major platforms are unavailable to users in large parts of the world due to regulatory constraints.
  • Complexity: Most platforms are designed for professional traders, with interfaces and product offerings that are inaccessible to the broader retail market.
  • Opacity: Pricing, settlement, and collateral mechanics are typically black boxes controlled by the exchange operator.

The Solution

BitOptions addresses these problems by delivering a fully on-chain, non-custodial options protocol. Users retain custody of their funds at all times. Smart contracts govern every aspect of option creation, premium collection, and settlement — eliminating the need to trust a centralized operator.

The protocol launches with weekly options on Bitcoin (BTC), Ethereum (ETH), and Solana (SOL), cash-settled in USDT. All contracts expire every Friday at 08:00 UTC.

Key Principles

  • Simplicity: Options in three clicks — pick asset, pick strike, pick call or put.
  • Accessibility: Permissionless participation; no KYC, no geographic blocks.
  • Transparency: Pricing formulas, pool balances, and settlement mechanics are fully on-chain and publicly auditable.
Section 02

Background: Options Fundamentals

2.1 What Are Options?

An option is a financial derivative that grants the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset at a predetermined price — the strike price — on or before a specified expiry date.

  • Call option: The right to buy the underlying asset at the strike price. Profits when the asset rises above the strike.
  • Put option: The right to sell the underlying asset at the strike price. Profits when the asset falls below the strike.

The buyer pays a one-time upfront cost called the premium to acquire this right. The seller (writer) receives the premium and takes on the obligation to fulfill the contract if the buyer exercises.

TermDefinition
Strike Price (K)The price at which the option can be exercised
Expiry (T)The date/time after which the option ceases to exist
PremiumThe price paid to acquire the option
In-the-money (ITM)When exercising would be profitable (call: S > K; put: S < K)
Out-of-the-money (OTM)When exercising would not be profitable
At-the-money (ATM)When spot price equals or is near the strike price

2.2 Option Pricing

Option premiums are determined by a combination of intrinsic value and time value. The most widely used analytical model is the Black-Scholes model (1973), which provides a closed-form solution for European option pricing.

Black-Scholes Call Price
C = S · N(d₁) − K · e−rT · N(d₂)
d₁ and d₂
d₁ = [ ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2) · T ] / (σ · √T)
d₂ = d₁ − σ · √T

Where the inputs are defined as:

  • S — Current spot price of the underlying asset
  • K — Strike price of the option
  • T — Time to expiry (in years)
  • r — Risk-free interest rate
  • σ — Implied volatility of the underlying asset
  • N(·) — Cumulative standard normal distribution function

The key sensitivity measures — collectively known as the Greeks — describe how the option price changes relative to each input:

GreekSymbolSensitivity ToDescription
DeltaΔSpot price (S)Change in option price per $1 move in the underlying
GammaΓDelta (rate of change)Rate of change of Delta; highest near-the-money
ThetaΘTime to expiry (T)Time decay — option loses value as expiry approaches
VegaνImplied volatility (σ)Change in option price per 1% change in volatility

2.3 Weekly Options

BitOptions exclusively offers weekly-expiring options. Weekly expirations offer several practical advantages over monthly or quarterly contracts:

  • Flexibility: Traders can express short-term views without committing to longer-dated positions.
  • Event plays: Weekly contracts are well-suited to trade around scheduled events (CPI releases, Fed meetings, protocol upgrades).
  • Reduced theta exposure: Shorter duration means faster time decay, which benefits option sellers and creates cleaner risk profiles for buyers.

BitOptions uses European-style cash settlement in USDT rather than physical delivery. At expiry, the smart contract calculates each option's intrinsic value using the oracle settlement price and automatically credits the payout to the buyer's wallet. The seller (liquidity pool) pays the payout from locked collateral.

BitOptions is a smart-contract-based options protocol deployed on the Solana blockchain. Solana was selected for its high throughput, sub-second finality, and low transaction fees — all essential properties for a real-time derivatives platform.

3.3 Settlement

At expiry, the settlement price is determined by the Pyth Network oracle using a 30-minute TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) window ending at the scheduled expiry time. The TWAP window prevents last-minute price manipulation that could otherwise artificially push options in or out of the money.

Call Option Payout
Payoutcall = max(0, ST − K) × contract_size
Put Option Payout
Payoutput = max(0, K − ST) × contract_size

Where ST is the TWAP settlement price and K is the option's strike price. Smart contracts automatically calculate and distribute payouts to buyers' wallets at expiry — no manual claim or exercise is required.

Section 04

Liquidity Pool Architecture

4.1 The Shared Liquidity Pool

Each supported asset has a dedicated shared USDT liquidity pool that acts as the sole counterparty to all option buyers. When a trader buys an option, they pay a premium into the pool; when the option expires in-the-money, the pool pays out the settlement amount.

  • Liquidity providers (LPs) deposit USDT into the pool and receive LP tokens representing their proportional share.
  • LP tokens are fungible and transferable. Their value increases as premium income accumulates.
  • The pool is collectively short options — it collects premium income and bears the risk of adverse price moves.

4.2 LP Token Mechanics

LP Token Price
LP Token Price = Total Pool Value / Total LP Token Supply
Deposit — Tokens Minted
LP Tokens Minted = Deposit Amount / Current LP Token Price
Withdrawal — USDT Returned
USDT Returned = LP Tokens × Current LP Token Price

Withdrawals are temporarily restricted when the pool's available capital falls below a minimum safety threshold, preventing a bank-run scenario during high-utilization periods.

4.3 Collateral & Capital Utilization

For every option sold, the protocol locks collateral equal to the maximum possible payout of that option. This ensures the pool can always honor its obligations at expiry, regardless of how far the asset moves.

Available Capital
Available Capital = Total Deposits − Locked Collateral
Utilization Rate
Utilization Rate = Locked Collateral / Total Deposits
💡 Target Utilization: The protocol is designed to operate between 40–60% utilization for optimal yield/risk balance. Dynamic pricing (Section 5) automatically deters over-concentration.

4.4 Premium Distribution

Option premiums paid by buyers flow directly into the pool at the moment of purchase. This immediately increases the LP token price, accruing value to all liquidity providers proportionally.

  • A platform fee is deducted from each premium and directed to the protocol treasury.
  • The fee rate is set by governance and is publicly viewable on-chain.
Net LP Yield (Period)
Net Yield = Σ(Premiums Collected) − Σ(Payouts Made) / Time Period
Section 05

Dynamic Pricing Model

5.1 Base Premium

The base premium for each option is derived from the Black-Scholes model using a volatility surface calibrated to realized market data. Implied volatility (σ) is updated periodically by the protocol, ensuring premiums reflect current market conditions.

Base Premium
Base Premium = BS(S, K, T, r, σbase)

Where σbase is the protocol's current implied volatility estimate for the asset, updated at regular intervals using on-chain and off-chain volatility data.

5.2 Imbalance Adjustment

As the pool approaches maximum utilization — indicating that demand for options is high relative to available capital — the protocol applies an Imbalance Multiplier (μ) to premiums. This serves a dual purpose: protecting LP capital at high utilization and providing a natural demand-limiting mechanism.

5.3 Size Impact

To prevent large orders from concentrating risk in the pool, a Size Impact Factor applies a pricing penalty proportional to the order's share of available pool capital.

Size Impact Factor
Size Impact = 1 + (Order Notional / Pool Available Capital) × 0.1

Small orders (notional well below available capital) experience negligible size impact. Large orders approaching pool capacity receive progressively worse pricing, naturally discouraging pool concentration.

Section 06

Risk Management

6.1 Notional Caps

Hard exposure limits are enforced at the smart contract level to prevent any single directional bet from overwhelming the pool:

  • Maximum one-directional exposure per asset: 40% of pool capital (calls or puts separately)
  • Maximum total per-asset exposure: 60% of pool capital (calls + puts combined)
  • Orders that would breach these caps are rejected at the contract level — not merely priced higher.

6.2 Oracle Security

Price oracle integrity is a core security property. BitOptions relies on Pyth Network as the primary price feed provider, chosen for its institutional-grade data aggregation and low-latency on-chain delivery on Solana.

  • The 30-minute TWAP at expiry provides strong resistance to short-term price manipulation attempts.
  • A secondary oracle fallback is activated automatically in the event of a primary feed outage, ensuring settlement can always proceed.
  • Oracle deviation monitors flag anomalous price movements for governance review.

6.3 Smart Contract Risk

  • All protocol contracts will undergo a full security audit by OtterSec prior to mainnet deployment.
  • A bug bounty program will be active post-launch to incentivize responsible vulnerability disclosure.
  • Contracts use an upgradeable proxy pattern with timelock governance, ensuring any upgrades require a 48-hour delay and on-chain governance approval before taking effect.

6.4 LP Risk Disclosure

⚠️ Risk Warning: Liquidity providers act as options sellers and bear the risk of large directional price moves. In a worst-case scenario — a sustained, large one-directional move in an underlying asset — the pool may experience significant losses.

The protocol mitigates tail risk through dynamic pricing escalation and hard notional caps, but these mechanisms do not eliminate risk entirely. Key LP considerations:

  • Dynamic pricing and notional caps limit maximum one-sided exposure.
  • Historical data across crypto derivatives markets shows option sellers are profitable in approximately 70–75% of weekly cycles, though past performance is not indicative of future results.
  • LPs should size positions appropriately and monitor pool utilization.
